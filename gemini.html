Rolling it out to a whole company: identity, data, guardrails and the people

An enterprise AI assistant is only partly a technical project. Half of it is plumbing that has to be right before anyone logs in, which identity provider, which data connectors, whose documents each person may see, what the model is allowed to say. The other half is getting several thousand people to actually use it. Twelve sections, plain English first, and simulations for the decisions that are hard to undo.

The machine behind this lab: a sorter that sends each badge down the right lane

In plain English: rolling an assistant out to a whole company starts with one decision that is hard to undo: which identity system signs people in. Here that decision is a sorter with three lanes. The data you connect picks the lane. Google Workspace goes through Google’s own identity, Microsoft 365 through federation with Entra ID, and an automated pipeline, which is not a person, through its own workload identity.
